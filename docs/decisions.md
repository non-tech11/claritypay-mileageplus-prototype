# Product decisions in this build

1. **Loyalty appears once on discovery, in force at checkout.** Search shows one chip on the fare most likely to be financed; the full earn breakdown waits for the offer screen. Discovery earns attention, checkout earns conversion — over-selling loyalty at search erodes trust in the price.

2. **The lender is invisible except where the law requires it.** United branding everywhere the customer acts; ClarityPay appears only as the wallet micro-tag, the prequal disclosure block, the loan agreement, and statement footers. All regulated copy is a shared constant (`lib/copy.ts`) — disclosures are never free-typed.

3. **Rewards exist only where there is margin: APR plans.** A 0% plan earns nothing — the subsidised rate *is* the reward, and there is no margin to fund miles from. Monthly (APR) plans earn 1 mi/$ of fare, and Economy Plus adds 0.5 bonus mi/$ financed (capped), concentrating spend-up where the airline's margin is. The bonus is paid in full over a 12-month reference term and tapers beyond it, so the loyalty currency actively steers toward shorter debt (see decision 15). Enforced in the engine and unit-tested; the offer screen shows per-plan earn against APR and total cost so the trade-off is legible at the point of choice.

4. **Bonus goes to the payer only; base goes to every traveller.** One loan, one borrower, one bonus. Base miles are earned on the fare and split per traveller with a MileagePlus number; travellers without one get a 30-day retro-credit window instead of silently losing the earn.

5. **ClarityPay funds all reward miles (purchased from the airline), but the two types keep different lifecycles.** Trip miles credit after the flight, are reversed only if the booking is refunded, and delinquency never touches them — they anchor trust in the reward. The bonus credits **when the plan completes** — completion rewards repayment, there is nothing to claw back mid-loan, and a cured delinquency simply restores it to pending (held at 30 DPD, reversed at 60). The funder is invisible to the customer: the reward carries the airline's brand end to end, like a cobrand card, additional to whatever the member earns for flying.

6. **No cash clawback for miles, ever.** Redeemed-then-cancelled reverses what the balance covers and books the shortfall as a "miles owed" line that nets against future earning. Converting a loyalty problem into a surprise charge would poison both brands.

7. **Declines are soft and recoverable in one tap.** No reason codes beyond the legal minimum, adverse-action notice by mail, fallback to card without re-entering anything — and the loyalty line says plainly that miles are a payment-plan benefit, so the card fallback earns none.

8. **Loyalty failure never blocks lending.** If the MileagePlus posting API is down, the loan proceeds, the customer sees "pending — retrying, posts within 72 hours", and United ops gets an exception queue item with a retry button. Money movement and points movement are decoupled by design.

9. **The whole loyalty layer is config-driven for reusability.** Theme config (`lib/theme.ts`) re-skins every widget — `/?theme=demo-retailer` proves it with a generic points program. Program economics (`/api/merchant/config`) are editable from the dashboard and apply to checkout on next load.

10. **Chart is hand-rolled SVG, state is in-memory, personas replace auth.** Prototype-scale choices made deliberately: no chart dependency, no database (documented cold-start reset + `POST /api/reset`), and a persona switcher that also drives underwriting outcomes so every path is walkable without credentials.

11. **One plan is recommended, and it's the 12-month.** A default fights choice paralysis; 12 months balances a manageable payment against total cost, and (unlike 0%) it earns miles — aligned incentives for customer, airline, and ClarityPay.

12. **Partial cancellation keeps the payer's bonus.** The bonus rewards the financed booking (which still exists, re-amortised), not headcount. The cancelled traveller's trip miles are reversed pro-rata; the loan is re-amortised so unpaid instalments sum to the new outstanding balance.

13. **The earn model is final as built: 1 mi/$ of fare, 0.5 bonus mi/$ financed, and 0% earns nothing.** Base is 1 mi/$ of fare excluding taxes; the pay-over-time bonus is 0.5 mi/$ financed, capped at 1,000, on Economy Plus fares only, and tapered by term (decision 15). A 0% plan writes no ledger entries at all — its subsidised rate is already the reward, and there is no margin left to fund miles on top of it. This supersedes the flat "500 bonus per booking / base 5 mi/$" sketch in the original build plan (`docs/PLAN.md`), which was written before the unit economics were worked through.

14. **"or from $X/mo" quotes the cheapest monthly instalment, not the recommended plan.** `estimateMonthly` (`lib/engine/loan.ts`) returns the lowest 30-day-interval payment on the prime ladder — the 24-month plan — so the headline is a genuine "from" price. The plan pre-selected one tap later is the 12-month (decision 11), whose payment is higher. The figure is also computed on the prime ladder for everyone, because it is shown before prequal: a near-prime customer sees the prime "from" price until the offer page quotes their real terms.

15. **The bonus tapers with term, so the reward stops being neutral to risk.** It is paid in full over a 12-month reference term (`bonusReferenceTermMonths`) and in proportion beyond it, so a 24-month plan earns half what a 12-month plan earns on the same booking. The previous rule — identical bonus on every APR term — meant ClarityPay paid the same reward cost for double the exposure while the customer got no reward reason to choose the shorter plan. On a $416.80 booking the 24-month option already costs $81.44 in interest against $34.16 for 12 months, to defer a payment under $40; the loyalty currency should not be indifferent to that. This supersedes the term-independence claim in decision 3. The taper only ever reduces: a plan shorter than the reference term earns the full rate, never a multiplied-up one.

16. **Pay-in-4 collects the first instalment at checkout; the monthly plans stay zero-down.** The market has already split on this line — Klarna and Afterpay take 25% at the till because on a small ticket the down payment *is* the underwriting, while Affirm's monthly loans take nothing, because "finance the whole purchase" is the value proposition and asking for cash there sends the customer back to their card. Requiring a down payment across all three tenures would cost share of wallet exactly where the financing decision is most deliberate. Collection happens at **signing**, not plan selection: choosing a plan is not consent, so charging any earlier would debit a customer who has agreed to nothing. The offer page states the amount due today before the customer commits, and the CTA reads "Pay and book" rather than "Sign and book" on that plan.

17. **No credit terms before underwriting.** The cart previously opened a plan sheet listing all three plans with their APRs, totals and per-plan earn, and checkout then echoed "Plan chosen: 12 monthly payments" — all of it before any decision had been made. Two things were wrong with that. Commercially it was a lie for anyone but a prime customer: the sheet built its ladder on a hardcoded prime profile, so a near-prime shopper picked a 24-month plan that silently disappeared once their real two-plan ladder came back. Legally, quoting a specific monthly payment is a triggering term, and presenting a selectable ladder reads as an offer that does not exist yet. The sheet is gone; plans now exist only on the offer screen, sourced from the approved offer. What survives pre-underwriting is a single "or from $X/mo" figure, explicitly labelled a representative example and no longer a link, which is what Affirm and Klarna show on a product page. That keeps the conversion hook without representing terms as available.
